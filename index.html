Signal Integrity & High-Speed Digital Design

Seventeen decks in five sections on getting a signal from one chip to another intact — the physics of the channel, the mechanisms that close an eye, and the arithmetic a standard uses to decide a channel is legal.

Every number on every slide is computed by a model in si_models and embedded as data. Nothing is asserted by hand, and the models are checked against published worked examples wherever one exists.

Long-form companion

The same material as a written report: Signal_Integrity.pdf. It carries the continuous argument where the decks carry the interactive models, and it is generated from the same computations, so a number cannot differ between the two.

How to read the series

  • Decks 01–04 build the passive channel: what a transmission line is, where the return current flows, what the materials do to the signal, and what happens at the one feature that is made by drilling.
  • Decks 05–06 are about the signal on it — why it is differential, and the one impairment no equaliser can remove.
  • Decks 07–10 are about jitter — the quantity a serial link is finally judged on, and the one most often mis-stated.
  • Decks 11–14 are about power integrity, ending with the four routes by which a disturbance on a rail reaches the eye.
  • Deck 15 assembles a timing budget; decks 16–17 ask whether any of it can be believed, and how a standard turns it into a verdict.

Most decks are worked against one channel — the 28.8 inch backplane characterised in Equalisation in High-Speed Serial Links — so the numbers in one deck can be set against the numbers in another.
